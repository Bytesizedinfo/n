A day at Stillwater

Slow mornings. Early nights.

There's no timetable to keep, only a rhythm to fall into. This is how most days go.

  1. 06:00

    Sunrise breathwork

    On the jetty, wrapped in blankets, as the mist lifts off the water.

  2. 07:30

    Cold-water swim

    Always optional. Tam swims beside you.

  3. 08:30

    Breakfast

    Porridge, mere-smoked trout and rye from our own oven.

  4. 11:00

    Silent walk

    Along the old road, to the place where it disappears into the water.

  5. 15:00

    Sauna and rest

    Sleep, read, or sit and watch the water. Guests find it surprisingly hard to look away.

  6. 19:00

    Supper by the fire

    Shared tables. You'll find you have more in common with the others than you expected.

  7. 21:00

    Lights out

    Please close your shutters after dark and keep them closed until morning.

  8. 03:00

    Listening

    Some guests hear bells from the water. This is normal. Do not answer them.

  9. 03:33

    The Call

    Stay in your cabin unless you hear your name.

Getting here

Easy to find. Hard to leave.

By car
Leave the motorway at junction 14 and follow signs for Fenmoor. After the cattle grid the road narrows. Follow it until it stops. Then keep walking.
By train
The nearest station is Ravensby, 38 miles away. We'll collect you. We always collect you.
Good to know
The last fuel station is 40 miles out, and there's no phone signal past Ravensby Gill.

Our mere

The story of Hollin Mere.

Every guest asks about the water. This is the account kept in the parish register.

Parish of St. Hollin, Fenmoor Register of Events · 1891
Hollin
Green

In the spring of 1891 the Fenmoor Water Board completed its dam at Ravensby Gill, and the valley of Hollin Green was flooded to supply the towns below. The village had stood for six hundred years. Its people were offered new houses in Ravensby, and most accepted.

The congregation of St. Hollin's did not. On the evening the sluices were closed, forty-one parishioners gathered in the church under Agnes Merrow, sister of the curate. They told the Board's men they had chosen stillness, and that they would wait for the water together. The water reached the windows at a quarter past nine. They were still singing.

No bodies were recovered. Agnes Merrow walked out of the mere three days later, dry. That autumn she founded a house on the new shore, so that the parish would never be without company.

On still days the spire can be seen beneath the surface. The service has not ended.

Questions

Before you arrive.

Anything else, ask Wren on chat.

What should I pack?

Warm layers, swimwear and walking boots. Leave your laptop at home; there's nowhere to plug it in.

Is there phone signal?

No. Phones are handed in when you arrive and kept safe in the boathouse until you leave.

Do I have to swim?

No. But most guests find they want to after the third night.

Can I leave early?

Why would you want to?

What are the bells I can hear at night?

There are no bells at Hollin Mere. The nearest church is eleven miles away.

Who is the woman on the jetty?

There is no woman on the jetty.
